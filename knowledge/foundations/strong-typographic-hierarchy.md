# Strong Typographic Hierarchy

## Summary

Establishing clear reading order through decisive contrasts in type scale, weight, spatial grouping, and tonal separation.

## Visual or Cinematic Principle

Every text layer should occupy a distinct perceptual tier—primary anchor, structural signpost, supporting body, and quiet metadata—so a viewer can grasp structure at a glance before reading sentence by sentence.

## Why It Works

Pre-attentive visual processing detects large shifts in scale, weight, and luminance before semantic reading begins. Decisive ratios (such as 2.5x or greater between display and body sizes) remove ambiguity about where to look first.

## Practical Application

- Limit a poster or editorial layout to two or three distinct typographic voices or tiers rather than inventing slight size variations for every element.
- Pair tight line-height and deliberate tracking on large display headings with generous leading on small body copy.
- Use tonal value (opacity or ink step) alongside scale so secondary metadata recedes cleanly without shrinking below legible limits.

## Constraints

- Excessive contrast without spatial alignment creates visual fragmentation.
- Overly tight display tracking on small screens or low-DPI media harms legibility.

## Common Mistakes

- Creating subtle 1–2px size differences that look accidental rather than hierarchical.
- Relying exclusively on color shifts without structural spacing or scale differences.
